# Pre-registered question 1, not answerable at this scale: which retrieval knob matters most?

Sources: reports/sweep_results.md (generated by scripts/analyze_sweep.py), logs/sweep/*.jsonl, plan in rag/sweep_plan.py
(written before any result). Titan v2 embeddings, exact cosine retrieval, 9 one-factor-at-a-time configs. The baseline is 512-token chunks,
measured overlap 8.2% (nominal 10%), top-5.

## Result

Retrieval on this corpus sits near its ceiling, and **no knob moves recall beyond its confidence interval.**

Tier A main pool (n = 33, sqrt-allocated across the corpus), recall at the config's k, with bootstrap 95% CIs:

| config | recall@k | above random | MRR@k |
|---|---|---|---|
| base (512 / 8.2% / k5) | 88 [76, 97] | 75 [63, 85] | 0.73 |
| 256-token chunks | 88 [76, 97] | 74 [62, 84] | 0.71 |
| 1024-token chunks | 88 [76, 97] | 76 [64, 85] | 0.72 |
| overlap 0% | 85 [70, 97] | 72 [59, 82] | 0.65 |
| overlap 22.9% (nominal 25%) | 85 [73, 97] | 72 [58, 83] | 0.68 |
| top-3 | 79 [64, 91] | 71 [56, 83] | 0.71 |
| top-10 | 91 [82, 100] | 67 [55, 75] | 0.73 |

Overlap is reported as MEASURED; nominal values are in parentheses.

- **Largest change from base, Tier A:** top-k −9 points (top-3), overlap −3, chunk size 0. The top-k effect is
  mostly mechanical: three slots instead of five. Raw recall and recall above random give the same ranking.
- **Tier B (n = 13):** the two rankings **disagree**. By raw recall: top-k (+23, at k = 10) > chunk size (+15) >
  overlap (+8). Above random: chunk size (+17) > overlap (+9) > top-k (+5). The disagreement was pre-registered as
  reportable. Its cause: Tier B's gold sections are large, so chance@10 is 42%, and most of top-10's raw gain is
  what random retrieval would also get. On Tier B one question is 8 points, and every one of these deltas sits
  inside its CI.
- **Interaction check (the 256-token pair):** the direct and extrapolated chunk-size effects differ by under 1
  point. As pre-registered, this is ONE observation (the 1024 pair differs in only 2 of 64 chunks), not a
  demonstration that OFAT's no-interaction premise holds.

## Why it cannot be answered here, and what would answer it

- **The ceiling.** The base config already retrieves 29 of 33 Tier A questions, so no knob can gain more than 4
  questions (12 points). Exact McNemar at α = 0.05 needs at least 6 discordant questions in one direction. At
  n = 33, **no improvement over base is detectable at all**: the power to detect a 5-point gain is 0.005.
- **The number needed.** Detecting a 5-point gain at an 88% baseline with 80% power needs about **160 questions**
  if the knob only ever helps, and about **400** with realistic churn (3% of questions lost while 8% gain). An
  eval set hard enough to start near 60% would need far fewer. This corpus (33,757 tokens, 46 labelled sections)
  does not have room for either.
- **The answer to the operational question:** at the scale of a single project's documentation, chunking choices
  within 256-1024 tokens and 0-25% overlap do not matter measurably, and top-k matters mainly through the number of
  slots.

## Answers and citations (Haiku 4.5, the same prompt for every config)

- **Citation of a gold section, Tier A main:** 79-88% across configs. Valid citations (every cited label is a
  retrieved chunk) are 96-100%.
- **Abstention on Tier B's no-gold questions:**
  - B02 (unanswerable) abstains in 9 of 9 configs.
  - B06 (unsettled) abstains in 9 of 9 **on reading the answers**, though the detector reports 8 of 9.

  The miss is an instrument error: the abstention phrases are English-only, and at cs256 the model abstained in
  Chinese ("無法確定…沒有說明"). Both are measured under an explicit abstention instruction, so they are upper bounds
  on unprompted behaviour.
- **One wrong abstention on an answerable question:** B13 at cs256_matched.
- **Latency:** p50 1.2-1.4 s per answer, in-region, Haiku 4.5.
